# Persuasion and consumer behaviour

Reference for `marketing:content-marketing`. Principles of influence and decision biases, each with an honest use and the manipulative version to refuse. Persuasion that relies on false scarcity, fake reviews or hidden costs is a dark pattern; it breaks consumer-protection law in many countries (in Brazil, the Código de Defesa do Consumidor) and damages trust.

## Cialdini's principles of influence

| Principle | Mechanism | Honest use | Refuse |
|---|---|---|---|
| Reciprocity | People return favours | Genuinely useful free content, trials, tools | Gifts that create obligation before disclosure |
| Commitment and consistency | People act in line with what they said or did | Small voluntary first steps; let users set their own goals | Pre-ticked boxes, foot-in-the-door traps |
| Social proof | People follow others like them | Real reviews, usage numbers, named customers with permission | Invented testimonials, inflated counts |
| Authority | People defer to credible expertise | Verifiable credentials, sources, certifications | Fake experts, borrowed logos |
| Liking | People say yes to those they like and resemble | Clear, warm, relevant communication | Impersonation, fake personal messages |
| Scarcity | Limited things seem more valuable | State real stock, deadlines or capacity | Fake countdowns, perpetual "last units" |
| Unity (added in *Pre-Suasion*, 2016) | Shared identity increases influence | Real community and shared values | Exploiting group identity to exclude or pressure |

## Decision biases relevant to marketing

| Effect | Origin | Honest use | Watch out |
|---|---|---|---|
| Anchoring | Tversky and Kahneman | Show a reference price that is real (the regular price, the plan most choose) | Fictitious "was" prices |
| Loss aversion | Kahneman and Tversky (prospect theory) | Show what a delay really costs | Fear-based copy about unlikely losses |
| Framing | Tversky and Kahneman | Present data in the frame the customer decides in (per month, per use) | Frames that hide the total |
| Decoy (asymmetric dominance) | Huber, Payne and Puto | Clarify trade-offs between plans | Plans that exist only to push another |
| Endowment effect | Thaler | Trials and personalisation that create real ownership | Hard-to-cancel trials |
| Confirmation bias | — | Address existing beliefs with evidence | Selective evidence |
| Halo effect | Thorndike | Lead with the quality customers care about most | Implying unproven qualities |
| Mere exposure | Zajonc | Consistent, repeated brand presence | Frequency so high it irritates |
| Hyperbolic discounting | — | Deliver an immediate benefit for long-term behaviours | Pushing debt or costly deferred payments |
| Choice overload | Iyengar and Lepper | Recommend a default and fewer, clearer options | Effect varies across studies; test it |

## Fogg behaviour model

Behaviour happens when **motivation**, **ability** and a **prompt** converge at the same moment (B = MAP). If a desired action does not happen, check in this order: is there a prompt at the right time; is the action easy enough (time, money, effort, routine); only then try to raise motivation. Making the action easier is usually cheaper and more durable than persuading harder.

## Copy checklist

- Every claim is true and can be substantiated; numbers have a source and a date.
- Scarcity, urgency and social proof are real and current.
- The total cost and the conditions are visible before commitment; cancelling is as easy as signing up.
- The message works without the bias: biases support a good offer, they do not replace one.

When these effects show up in an interface (defaults, choice architecture, cancellation flows), review them with `ux:usability-review`; the related laws (choice overload, Von Restorff, peak-end) are in its `references/heuristics-and-laws.md`.
