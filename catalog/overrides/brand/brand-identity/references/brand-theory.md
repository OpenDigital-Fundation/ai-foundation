# Brand theory: equity, identity, positioning and adoption

Reference for `brand:brand-identity`. Models to structure brand work. Most are conceptual frameworks rather than tested laws; use them to make choices explicit, and check the choices with category buyers (see `marketing:campaign-planning`, `references/marketing-science.md` for the empirical regularities of brand growth).

## Brand equity

| Model | Author | Components | Use |
|---|---|---|---|
| Brand equity | David Aaker | Awareness, perceived quality, associations, loyalty (and other proprietary assets) | Decide which component the brand is weakest on |
| Brand identity system | David Aaker | Core identity, extended identity, value proposition; brand as product, organisation, person and symbol | Write the identity the brand aspires to, separately from its current image |
| Customer-based brand equity (CBBE) pyramid | Kevin Lane Keller | Salience → performance and imagery → judgements and feelings → resonance | Build from the bottom: no resonance without salience |
| Brand identity prism | Jean-Noël Kapferer | Physique, personality, culture, relationship, reflection, self-image | Check that sender-side (physique, personality) and receiver-side (reflection, self-image) facets agree |
| Brand personality | Jennifer Aaker | Sincerity, excitement, competence, sophistication, ruggedness | Describe the intended personality with research-backed dimensions |
| Brand archetypes | Margaret Mark and Carol Pearson | Twelve archetypes (innocent, explorer, sage, hero, outlaw, magician, regular person, lover, jester, caregiver, creator, ruler) | Shared vocabulary for tone and narrative; not a substitute for positioning |
| Zag and the brand gap | Marty Neumeier | Bridge strategy and creativity; radical differentiation | Workshops on differentiation |

## Positioning

**Positioning** (Al Ries and Jack Trout) is the place a brand holds in the minds of the target buyers relative to competitors. A positioning statement names the target, the frame of reference (category), the point of difference and the reason to believe.

*The 22 Immutable Laws of Marketing* (Ries and Trout) offers maxims, not laws: leadership (being first in a category helps), category (create a category you can lead), mind (being first in the mind beats being first in the market), perception (marketing is a battle of perceptions), focus (own a word), exclusivity (two brands cannot own the same word), opposite (a challenger can position against the leader), line extension (stretching a name weakens it), candour (admitting a negative can earn trust), and acceleration (build on trends, not fads). Several conflict with evidence from brand-growth research (for example, the importance of reaching all category buyers rather than a narrow niche); test before relying on them.

**Golden Circle** (Simon Sinek): communicate why, then how, then what. Useful for messaging hierarchy; the neuroscience claims made for it are not supported.

## Adoption of innovations

| Model | Author | Content | Use |
|---|---|---|---|
| Diffusion of innovations | Everett Rogers | Adopter categories: innovators (~2.5%), early adopters (~13.5%), early majority (~34%), late majority (~34%), laggards (~16%); adoption speed depends on relative advantage, compatibility, complexity, trialability and observability | Plan launch audiences and reduce complexity and risk of trial |
| Crossing the chasm | Geoffrey Moore | Technology products stall between early adopters and the pragmatic early majority | Win one niche segment completely (the "beachhead") with a whole product before expanding |

## Applying the models

1. Audit the current image with buyers (awareness, associations, personality) before writing the target identity.
2. Write the identity with one model (Kapferer or Aaker), the positioning statement, and the distinctive assets to build.
3. Check consistency across touchpoints with `brand:brand-voice` and the design system.
4. Track brand health over time: awareness among category buyers, associations with category entry points, distinctive-asset recognition.
