# Legal design

## Establish the source and user task

Identify the authoritative document/version, jurisdiction and effective date when relevant, intended audience, context of use and the decision/action the reader must take. Use de-identified examples where possible. If the legal source is missing or disputed, label the output as a proposed structure and leave substantive questions for qualified review.

Use legal-compliance:contracts for substantive contract drafting/redlines and legal-compliance:compliance-programs for control mapping. This skill owns the faithful, usable representation of legal information and its journey, not a legal opinion or a change in rights.

## Preserve meaning while changing presentation

1. Build a source map of rights, obligations, actors, triggers, deadlines, conditions, exceptions, definitions and consequences. Keep source clause/page identifiers and uncertainty visible. Read [the fidelity and comprehension protocol](references/fidelity-and-testing.md) for an original template and example.
2. Map the reader's task and decision points. Use plain-language headings, a clear information hierarchy, progressive detail and a text-equivalent path for diagrams. Preserve access to the complete authoritative text.
3. Rewrite one representative section. Keep who must/may act, negation, quantities, exceptions and references intact. Do not silently turn permission into obligation, a conditional right into an unconditional one or a proposal into the governing text.
4. Represent timelines only when the source supplies the triggering event, units and counting rule. If those are missing, preserve the literal rule and ask for verification instead of inventing a due date. For an asserted legal rule, verify current primary authority for the actual jurisdiction and date, or mark it unverified for professional review.
5. Compare the representation against the source map, then obtain a qualified review of meaning before relying on it in a real legal process. Separate a suggested change to a legal term from a presentational paraphrase.
6. Propose a comprehension test: ask representative readers to find who acts, what action is required, what triggers it and what exception applies. Observe task completion, misinterpretation and uncertainty without supplying the answer. A favourable usability result does not establish legal validity or enforceability.

## Deliverable

Source/version and audience, mapped clauses, prototype or rewritten sample, fidelity comparison, open legal questions and proposed user-test protocol/results clearly separated. Identify the authoritative version and reviewer decision. Do not file, sign, publish, replace official documents or assert qualified legal approval from a readability score.

Use ux:user-research for broader research and accessibility:document-accessibility for format-specific checks when available. Keep a readable text route when those capabilities are unavailable.
