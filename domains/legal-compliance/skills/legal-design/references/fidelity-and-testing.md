# Fidelity before readability

## Original source-to-design record

| Source locator/version | Actor and rule | Trigger/time condition | Exception | Proposed representation | Reviewer question |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Identify the exact clause | Preserve must/may, rights and obligations | Record event, quantity and counting rule exactly as supplied | Keep exclusions and cross-references | Plain-language block, table, form or diagram with text equivalent | Any ambiguity or proposed change in meaning |

Do not replace a missing source rule with a customary default. Marking a statement as unverified is different from inventing a legal restriction to simplify a flow.

## Original fictional example

Source: “The recipient may request a review within 10 days after receipt, except where Appendix A excludes that category.” This is a fictional clause for testing, not law or a reusable deadline.

A faithful summary preserves permission, the receipt trigger, the literal 10-day period and the Appendix A exception. It links the full clause and appendix and flags that the source does not state calendar/business days or a counting convention. It does not compute a date, say that review is mandatory, or remove the exception from the flow.

For a first prototype, use three visible questions: Is this category excluded by Appendix A? Has receipt occurred? Do you want to request a review? Provide an uncertainty branch for missing information. Have a qualified reviewer confirm whether that representation preserves the governing meaning before real use.

## Original test protocol

Recruit readers relevant to the actual task, with consent and only the minimum necessary information. Use a fictional scenario if real matters would expose private facts. Ask readers to locate the rule, describe the available choice and identify missing information. Record their interpretation and evidence location; do not coach them during the attempt. Classify errors by legal consequence as well as frequency. Revise a high-consequence ambiguity even when most readers completed the task. Report sample/context limits; do not turn a small pilot into a population-wide validity claim.
