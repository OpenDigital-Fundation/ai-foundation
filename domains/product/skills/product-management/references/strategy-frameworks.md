# Strategy frameworks

Reference for `product:product-management`. Frameworks for deciding where to compete and how to win, at company, portfolio and product level. Each one organises evidence; none produces a strategy without market data, and each has known blind spots.

## Analysing the environment and the industry

| Framework | Question it answers | Inputs | Blind spot |
|---|---|---|---|
| PESTEL | Which political, economic, social, technological, environmental and legal forces affect us? | Regulation, macro data, trends | Becomes a list; rank each force by impact and likelihood |
| Porter's five forces | How attractive is the industry: rivalry, threat of new entrants, threat of substitutes, buyer power, supplier power? | Industry structure, margins, switching costs | Static; complements (and platforms) are not one of the forces |
| SWOT | What are our internal strengths and weaknesses and external opportunities and threats? | Outputs of the analyses above | Easy to fill, hard to act on; turn it into a TOWS matrix of actions |
| 3Cs (Ohmae) | Company, customers, competitors: where do they meet? | Customer research, competitor analysis | Ignores collaborators and context |
| Scenario planning (Schwartz) | How would our strategy fare in several plausible futures? | Two key uncertainties crossed into four scenarios | Scenarios are not forecasts; define signposts to watch |

## Choosing a position

| Framework | Idea | Use |
|---|---|---|
| Porter's generic strategies | Cost leadership, differentiation or focus (on cost or differentiation in a niche) | Name the choice explicitly; being "stuck in the middle" is the usual failure |
| Value chain (Porter) | Primary activities (inbound logistics, operations, outbound logistics, marketing and sales, service) and support activities create value and cost | Find where advantage or cost actually sits |
| Resource-based view and VRIO (Barney) | Lasting advantage comes from resources that are valuable, rare, inimitable and organised to be exploited | Test each claimed advantage against all four |
| Blue Ocean (Kim and Mauborgne) | Create uncontested market space; the ERRC grid asks which factors to eliminate, reduce, raise and create | Draw the strategy canvas of the industry before redrawing yours |
| Value proposition canvas (Osterwalder) | Fit between customer jobs, pains and gains and the offer's pain relievers and gain creators | Product positioning and messaging; validate each assumption with customers |
| Jobs to be done | Customers hire a product to make progress in a situation | See `ux:user-research` (`references/jobs-and-journeys.md`) |

## Choosing growth paths and portfolio

| Framework | Axes | Use | Caveat |
|---|---|---|---|
| Ansoff matrix | Existing or new products × existing or new markets: penetration, market development, product development, diversification | Map growth options and their risk (diversification is the riskiest) | Says nothing about which option is attractive |
| BCG growth-share matrix | Market growth × relative market share: stars, cash cows, question marks, dogs | Balance cash across a portfolio | Assumes share drives cash; weak for new or network markets |
| GE–McKinsey matrix | Industry attractiveness × business strength, nine cells | Portfolio prioritisation with several criteria | Weighting is subjective; record the weights |
| Stakeholder matrix | Power × interest | Plan engagement: manage closely, keep satisfied, keep informed, monitor | Revisit as power shifts |
| McKinsey 7S | Strategy, structure, systems, shared values, style, staff, skills | Check that the organisation can execute the strategy | Diagnostic, not prescriptive |

## A working sequence

1. Environment: PESTEL and five forces, with sources.
2. Position: generic strategy, value chain and VRIO for the advantage you claim.
3. Options: Ansoff for growth paths, Blue Ocean for new space, portfolio matrices if there are several businesses.
4. Fit: value proposition canvas per segment, validated with customers.
5. Execution: 7S check, then OKR or Balanced Scorecard (`operations:project-management`, `references/management-models.md`).
6. Revisit with scenario signposts.
