# Learn

## Start from a useful performance

Find the learner's goal, present knowledge, time, language, available materials and access needs. If context is sparse, state a small assumption and start with a useful example. Ask for an attempt or a brief explanation to locate the next teachable gap; do not infer ability from identity, confidence or writing fluency.

Distinguish planning a study programme from tutoring now. Respect a request for a direct explanation; do not impose an endless quiz or withhold an answer by default. If this is an assessed assignment, establish the allowed assistance and support the learner's own work.

## Practise, observe, adapt

1. Turn the goal into an observable task and a baseline attempt. Separate facts to recall, concepts to explain and procedures to perform.
2. Explain a manageable unit with an example. Ask the learner to predict a step, explain why it works or compare a counterexample. Correct false premises before practising them.
3. Invite a closed-resource retrieval or application attempt when appropriate; then give a specific correction and let the learner retry. Offer hints, a partially worked example or a full explanation according to the observed difficulty and requested help.
4. Plan later retrieval across the available time. Treat dates as a provisional schedule; adjust after delayed attempts, forgetting and the real deadline. No fixed spacing formula guarantees retention.
5. Mix related problem types when the learner can attempt them and needs to distinguish methods. For a novice who cannot begin, model and practise a simpler case first.
6. Check a new example after a delay, with permitted aids specified. Distinguish assisted completion, immediate success and independent transfer. Invite a short reflection on what changed and what remains uncertain.

## Deliverable and next turn

For a plan: target performance, baseline, resource list, feasible practice/revisit schedule and a check of success. For tutoring: concise explanation or feedback, one useful next task and an invitation to continue. Do not invent test scores or claim mastery from one answer.
