# Instructional design

## Establish the design contract

Identify target learners, practical performance, prerequisites, context, delivery mode, time/workload, resources and access barriers. Clarify whether the goal is knowledge, procedural performance, judgement, collaboration or another outcome. Record unknowns as assumptions, not invented learner research.

## Design backward from evidence

1. Write a manageable set of observable outcomes: action, content, conditions and success criteria. Replace vague intentions such as “understand everything” with a performance that can be examined.
2. Use the revised Bloom taxonomy to describe the required cognitive process and knowledge type. Read [Bloom and alignment](references/bloom-and-alignment.md) for a two-dimensional map and an example. A verb alone cannot classify a task; inspect the information given, novelty and reasoning required. Not every course must end in Create or traverse every category.
3. Decide what evidence would support each outcome before selecting activities. A recall-only quiz does not establish design, evaluation or real-world transfer. Include the allowed aids and how the evidence will be reviewed.
4. Map each outcome to practice, feedback and assessment; find unsupported outcomes and activities with no relevant purpose. Sequence prerequisites, remove unnecessary load, model a difficult step and fade support according to observed performance.
5. Choose methods for the actual gap and constraints: retrieval/spaced revisit for needed knowledge, worked examples for an unfamiliar procedure, explanation/contrast for misconceptions, and a feasible problem/project for integration. These are choices to pilot, not guarantees of superior outcomes.
6. Design participation, representations and response options around access barriers while preserving the assessed construct. Budget learner and facilitator work, including feedback and transitions.
7. Pilot a small unit. Inspect evidence, errors, workload and access problems; revise the alignment rather than merely adding more content. Label proposed and observed results separately.

## Deliverable

Learner/context assumptions, outcome–evidence–practice matrix, sequence/workload, materials, feedback/revisit plan and pilot decision criteria. education:teach owns session facilitation; education:learning-assessment owns detailed assessment construction. A design document does not certify a course or demonstrate educational efficacy.
