# Learning assessment

## Define the inference before the instrument

Identify the supplied outcome, learners, assessment purpose, stakes, allowed resources, language/access needs and who is responsible for scoring. Use education:instructional-design if the outcomes or whole-course structure must first be designed. State what performance the result can and cannot support.

## Build and inspect the evidence

1. Map each outcome to a task and criterion, cognitive demand, coverage and workload. Include enough variety for the intended inference; one successful example cannot demonstrate broad mastery.
2. Match format to the construct: recall items for needed knowledge, explanations for reasoning, and authentic performances for application or design. A multiple-choice answer alone cannot prove a process the task never observes.
3. Write clear items with necessary context, defensible answers and plausible distractors based on likely errors. Check that answer cues, ambiguity, reading load or cultural assumptions are not testing something unintended. Verify subject-matter facts against reliable sources.
4. Create criterion-specific rubric descriptors that distinguish observable performance. Weight only what matters to the outcome. Read [the assessment playbook](references/assessment-playbook.md) for an original blueprint and sample rubric.
5. For formative use, return evidence-specific feedback, one achievable improvement and a retry. For summative/high-stakes use, require accountable human review and a clear process for moderation and appeal; do not submit official grades or certification automatically.
6. Pilot and calibrate with sample responses. Compare scorers and discuss discrepancies. Examine missingness, subgroup/access barriers and item behaviour only when data support it. An observed association is not proof of bias or validity.

## Output

Blueprint, tasks/items, answer rationale or scoring guide, rubric, accommodation assumptions and a review/revision plan. Separate proposed criteria from measured reliability/validity; do not fabricate coefficients, pass rates or cut scores. A passing quiz is evidence for its sampled tasks, not automatic competence in clinical, legal or other consequential practice.

Keep learner work private and use synthetic/de-identified samples for examples. education:learn owns personal practice; education:teach owns in-session teaching decisions.
