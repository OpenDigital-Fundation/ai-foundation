# Teach

## Frame the session

Identify the learners, intended performance, prior knowledge, session length, mode, language, group size, materials and access constraints. Name what learners should be able to do by the end and what observation would support it. If the request is for a whole course architecture, use education:instructional-design instead.

## Build a sequence learners can act on

1. Begin with a short diagnostic or prediction that makes relevant prior knowledge visible. Treat a wrong answer as evidence about a concept, not a trait of the learner.
2. Model the reasoning on a concrete example. Separate the key decision from incidental detail; pair a useful diagram with an accessible explanation when it helps the content.
3. Move to guided practice, then reduce support as attempts justify it. Use a contrasting case to expose a likely misconception. Do not replace all direct instruction with discovery for novices who lack the prerequisites.
4. Select participation for the goal: think–pair–share for comparing reasoning, peer explanation with an individual follow-up, a bounded problem/project for applying ideas, or short retrieval for necessary knowledge. Provide a route for learners who cannot speak publicly or use the default medium.
5. Insert checks that change the teaching decision: ask for reasoning or a small performance; specify when to reteach, model another example or move on. Silence, attendance and a thumbs-up are not sufficient evidence of understanding.
6. Close with a fresh application or exit question and an optional later revisit. Give actionable feedback about the work and a next attempt, avoiding public disclosure of individual difficulty.

## Produce a usable facilitation plan

Include objective, materials, timing ranges, facilitator actions, learner actions, likely misconception, check and adaptation branch. Ensure planned activity plus transition time fits the real session. Avoid claims that engagement alone proves learning, universal learning-style assignments or a single method suitable for every group.

Read [the session playbook](references/session-playbook.md) for a concrete plan and accessibility choices. Use education:learn for a learner's personal study loop; use education:learning-assessment for a substantial item bank or scoring rubric.
